# Gerd Gigerenzer

German psychologist known for work on decision heuristics, and for the position that
simple rules often outperform complex models in uncertain environments.

## Key facts

- Developed the **fast-and-frugal** framework by analysing real expert decisions rather
  than deriving rules from theory. See [[fast-and-frugal-heuristics]].
- Two studies Kennedy reports from *Gut Feelings* (2007): intensive care unit decisions
  about whether a patient is having a heart attack, and two UK magistrates' bail
  decisions. In both, a tree of three sequential questions captured the decision.
- Results: the medical decision tree outperformed both the heart disease predictive
  instrument and physicians; the bail tree explained 92% of the magistrates' decisions.

## Relevance

Fast-and-frugal trees are close to the ideal decision rule for an
[[agent-based-model]]: empirically grounded, computationally trivial, and readable. They
are the practical middle ground between an arbitrary [[threshold-rule]] and a full
[[cognitive-architecture]].
