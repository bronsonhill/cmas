# Herbert Simon

Economist, political scientist and cognitive scientist (1916–2001) who coined
**satisfice** — satisfy + suffice — in the 1950s and formulated
[[bounded-rationality]].

## Key facts

- Coined "satisfice" to name the behaviour of accepting an option that is good enough
  rather than searching for the optimum. See [[satisficing]].
- Bounded rationality: agents have limited information, limited cognitive ability and
  limited time, so the rational-choice ideal is unreachable in practice.
  Kennedy cites *The Sciences of the Artificial* (3rd ed., 1996) for it.
- Co-author with Allen Newell of *Human Problem Solving* (1972), the source of the
  brain-as-computer metaphor that shaped cognitive science and, later,
  [[cognitive-architecture|cognitive architectures]]. Kennedy's assessment is that the
  metaphor was both useful and distracting — useful for framing the mind as inputs,
  processes and outputs; distracting through its commitment to the von Neumann
  architecture, which drove the symbol-versus-connection debate.
- Nobel laureate in economics (1978) and Turing Award winner (1975).

## Relevance

Simon is the origin of the subject's central contrast between
[[optimisation]] and [[satisficing]] — the choice of decision rule that Week 5 puts at the
centre of designing agent behaviour, and that changes what an ABM concludes about
collective outcomes.
