# Influenza Antiviral Stockpile Model

A modelling study of how a limited national stockpile of antiviral drugs should be used
during an influenza pandemic. It is the Week 3 worked example of [[modelling-for-policy]],
and its conclusion fed into the Australian pandemic plan.

## Key facts

- **Problem:** reduce the impact of an influenza pandemic, given a stockpile too small to
  treat everyone.
- **Objectives:** delay the peak of the outbreak, and minimise the total number of
  infections.
- **Interventions compared:** using the drugs for prophylaxis (protecting the uninfected)
  versus for treatment (of those already infected).
- **Projections:** outbreak trajectories run across a range of epidemiological scenarios and
  intervention settings, presented as response surfaces of peak size against time and
  recovery probability.
- **Finding:** treatment alone was insufficient either to delay the peak or to reduce the
  number of infections, across the range of scenarios tested.
- **Implementation:** the finding was incorporated into the pandemic plan.

## Relevance

The study demonstrates each step of the policy pipeline on a real decision, and it makes two
points the lecture emphasised. First, the model is used only at the projection step —
objectives and the set of candidate interventions come from engagement with decision-makers,
not from the model. Second, a negative result is a usable result: establishing that treatment
alone does not achieve the objectives narrowed what the plan could rely on, which is the kind
of conclusion policy modelling exists to produce.

It also connects the abstract [[outbreak-summary-measures]] to real decisions. "Delay the
peak" and "minimise infections" are peak timing and final size, chosen as objectives because
each corresponds to something a health system can act on.
