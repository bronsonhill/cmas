# SARS 2003 Outbreak

The 2002–2004 outbreak of Severe Acute Respiratory Syndrome, centred on Hong Kong and
Southeast Asia. Used in the subject as the contained counterpart to
[[covid-19-pandemic]].

## Key facts

- Ran from late 2002 into mid-2003; the case data shown in lecture covers roughly four
  months from March 2003.
- Cumulative cases reached the order of 8,400 worldwide, with roughly 800 deaths.
- The daily case increments peaked and then dropped sharply following intervention around
  May 2003; the cumulative curve flattened correspondingly.
- Control measures in Hong Kong included closing schools and public facilities.
- SARS is classified as a critical disease because of its severity and transmissibility.

## Relevance to CMAS

The SARS curve is the lecture's demonstration that an [[epidemic-intervention]] is visible
in the data: the shape change is what a successful containment looks like, in contrast to
the [[exponential-growth]] of COVID-19. The question of what the 2003 response did that
worked was live for the modellers advising the 2020 Australian response — see
[[doherty-institute]] — which makes it a case where a historical outbreak informed the design
of a policy model.
