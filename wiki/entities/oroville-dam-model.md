# Oroville Dam Scale Model

A physical scale model of the Oroville Dam spillway, built to investigate why the spillway
failed. Used across both Week 1 lectures as the reference example of a physical
(non-computational) model.

## Key facts

- Built at reduced scale with real flowing water, to identify **points of failure** in the
  spillway.
- Because that was the purpose, it did not need to reproduce flow curves or surrounding
  rock precisely — which substantially reduced what it cost to build.
- Illustrates the general trade-off for physical simulation: such rigs demand space, money
  and ongoing maintenance. The University of Melbourne's wind tunnel in mechanical
  engineering is a comparable case — a large chamber with fans, used to test wind turbine
  and aircraft designs.
- The contrast drawn in lecture: computational modelling needs neither the space nor the
  budget, and a laptop is usually enough to run a worthwhile model.
- Source credit: *The New Humanitarian*, `https://bit.ly/3eXswj5`.

## Relevance to CMAS

Two lessons. First, that [[model|purpose]] governs required fidelity — knowing *where* it
breaks needs far less realism than predicting *how* it breaks. Second, it motivates the
subject's medium: computational models buy most of the insight at a tiny fraction of the
cost.
