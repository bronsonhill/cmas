# Mercator Projection

The Mercator projection is the standard flattened representation of the Earth's surface,
used here as a worked example of a model whose distortions are acceptable because they
serve its purpose.

## Key facts

- Projects the spherical Earth onto a rectangle, stretching the poles and compressing the
  equator.
- Represents landmass position and shape, altitude (mountains), ocean depth, and by
  extension vegetation, deserts and distances between locations.
- Its well-known flaw is **area distortion at high latitudes**: Greenland appears close to
  the size of Africa, which is far from true.
- Despite the distortion it remains near-universally used, because it is good enough for
  locating things — its actual purpose.
- Contrasted in lecture with earlier, cruder world maps from 500–600 years ago, which were
  adequate representations given the information then available, and with modern
  satellite/GIS/radar-derived elevation maps.

## Relevance to CMAS

A compact demonstration that a model can be visibly, quantifiably wrong in one respect and
still be the right model — because [[abstraction]] is judged against purpose, not against
fidelity. Useful as a counter to the instinct that a better model is always a more
accurate one.
