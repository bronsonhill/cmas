# Joshua M. Epstein

An American social scientist and one of the founders of agent-based social simulation. He
co-created [[sugarscape]] with Robert Axtell and argued for [[generative-explanation]] as the
epistemic contribution of [[agent-based-model]]s.

## Key facts

- Co-author of *Growing Artificial Societies: Social Science from the Bottom Up* (MIT Press,
  1996), with Robert Axtell.
- Author of *Generative Social Science* (Princeton, 2006), which sets out the "if you didn't
  grow it, you didn't explain it" position.
- Wrote *Why Model?* (JASSS, 2008), the essay set as reading for this subject
  ([[epstein-2008-why-model]]).
- Worked on the agent-based reconstruction of the Kayenta Anasazi in Long House Valley with
  Axtell and colleagues (PNAS, 2002) — a data-driven ABM of an ancient civilisation's growth
  and collapse.
- Funded through the NIH MIDAS project on infectious disease modelling at the time of the
  2008 essay; his applied work includes epidemic mitigation and containment.

## Relevance

His position — that the real choice is between explicit and implicit models, and that
prediction is one goal among sixteen — is the argument behind the subject's insistence that
a model needs a purpose and a [[research-question]] before it needs realism. The
science/policy split taught in [[w03a-analysing-models]] maps closely onto his list.
