# William G. Kennedy

Cognitive scientist and computational social scientist at the Krasnow Institute for
Advanced Study, George Mason University. Author of the chapter on modelling human
behaviour in *Agent-Based Models of Geographical Systems*.

## Key facts

- Works at the junction of cognitive modelling and agent-based social simulation — the
  question of what to put inside an agent that is meant to represent a person.
- His chapter surveys three tiers of approach (mathematical, conceptual frameworks,
  [[cognitive-architecture|cognitive architectures]]) and is unusually direct about the
  weaknesses of each.
- Best remembered for the opening argument: humans are not random, and modelling their
  choices as uniform draws is a strong, and usually wrong, empirical claim.

## Relevance

Kennedy supplies what the Week 5 lectures leave open. Muñoz sets up the ODD design
concepts — [[agent-sensing|sensing]], [[adaptive-behaviour|adaptation]],
[[objective-function|objectives]] — as slots to be filled;
[[kennedy-2012-modelling-human-behaviour]] is about what belongs in them when the agent is
