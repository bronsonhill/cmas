# Heliocentric Model

The heliocentric model places the Sun at the centre of the solar system, with the Earth and
planets in orbit around it. In lectures it is used, in contrast with the earlier geocentric
model, as the worked example of [[occams-razor]].

## Key facts

- The **geocentric** model put the Earth at the centre. To account for observed planetary
  motion — planets appearing to reverse direction across the sky — it required
  **epicycles**: small secondary orbits superimposed on the main orbits.
- The **heliocentric** model explains the same observations without epicycles. Moving the
  Sun to the centre makes the additional machinery unnecessary.
- Both models were built for the same purpose, which is what makes the comparison clean:
  the difference between them is complexity, not scope.
- Galileo's advocacy of heliocentrism brought him into serious conflict with the
  authorities of his time — noted in lecture as an aside.
- The simpler model did not merely tie with the complex one; it explained the planetary
  motion *more easily*.

## Relevance to CMAS

The subject's grounding example for preferring simpler models. It also carries a subtler
lesson: the epicycles were not an honest cost of detail but a symptom of a wrong structural
assumption. When a model needs ever more elaborate additions to fit observations, that is
evidence to re-examine its foundations — in [[modelling-cycle]] terms, to go back to steps
1 and 2 rather than forward.
