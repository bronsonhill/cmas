# COVID-19 Pandemic

The SARS-CoV-2 pandemic beginning in 2019. Used in the subject as the uncontained
counterpart to [[sars-2003-outbreak]] and as the motivating case for modelling disease
spread.

## Key facts

- Cumulative confirmed cases grew exponentially through the first half of 2020
  ([[exponential-growth]]).
- Australia closed its international borders for roughly 18 months, with mandatory
  quarantine for arrivals; reopening followed widespread vaccination.
- Australia's death toll was low by global standards; several European countries
  experienced far larger waves.
- Elimination did not occur. The disease is now endemic, and reinfection is possible — a
  direct violation of the permanent-immunity assumption in the basic [[sir-model]].
- Vaccines were rolled out from late 2020; one vaccine was subsequently found to carry rare
  adverse effects, which disrupted the rollout.
- Aged-care facilities were a locus of infection and fatality in Australia, and younger
  people were considered less susceptible.

## Relevance to CMAS

COVID-19 supplies the policy questions the lecture uses to motivate modelling: why this
outbreak spread when SARS was contained, when borders could safely reopen, whether
elimination was achievable, and why national experiences diverged. It also supplies the list
of factors that make the dynamics a [[complex-system]] — pathogen mutation, transmission
mode, individual susceptibility, global transport, social behaviour, interventions,
misinformation and politics. Agent-based models informed the Australian response through
the [[doherty-institute]]; see [[covid-19-abm-australia]].
