# Conway's Game of Life

Conway's Game of Life is the 2D binary [[cellular-automaton]] invented by British
mathematician John Conway in 1970, described conceptually on [[game-of-life]].

## Key facts

- Invented in 1970 by John Conway, inspired by [[john-von-neumann]]'s earlier work on
  self-replicating machines.
- Four rules based on the Moore (8-cell) neighbourhood govern each cell's alive/dead state
  each generation.
- Produces stable still-lifes, periodic oscillators, and mobile patterns (gliders).
- Is [[turing-completeness|Turing complete]], like elementary CA rule 110.

## Relevance

Life is the subject's worked 2D-CA example, used to demonstrate that [[emergence]] and
[[self-organisation]] require nothing beyond a grid, two states, and a handful of local
counting rules — see [[game-of-life]] for the full concept treatment.
