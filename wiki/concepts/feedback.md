# Feedback

Feedback is a loop in which a system's output influences its own future input, either
amplifying or dampening fluctuations in system state.

## How it works

**Positive feedback** amplifies fluctuations: the more of something there is, the faster
it grows. Exponential growth is the canonical example — the same dynamic behind the early,
uncontained growth phase of the [[sir-model]]. **Negative feedback** dampens fluctuations,
pulling the system back toward a bound; a system settling into a steady-state plateau after
initial growth is typically a signature of negative feedback checking what would otherwise
be unbounded increase.

## Why it matters

Feedback is one of the mechanisms by which [[decentralisation|decentralised]], locally-
interacting components produce coherent macroscopic dynamics without central direction —
it is a local signal (more of X leads to more, or less, of X) that aggregates into global
regime changes such as exponential outbreaks or stable equilibria.
